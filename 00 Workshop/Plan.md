# The Plan

**What I'm teaching:** one repeatable workflow, Extract, then Verify, then Synthesize, for turning a stack of papers into a synthesis you can trust. The output isn't a one-off summary, it's a note that lives in your own notes vault (Obsidian, or whatever you already use) and links to every other paper you've read, so it compounds over the whole fellowship instead of disappearing into a chat log.

**Why Obsidian specifically, not just "use AI":** Obsidian notes are plain markdown files, so nothing about this workflow locks a fellow into a tool they don't already use. But if you do use it (or a similar linked-notes app), each paper becomes a note with `[[wikilinks]]` to the papers it agrees with, disagrees with, or shares a research question with. By week 10, a fellow isn't holding a pile of disconnected AI chat summaries, they have a searchable, linked map of everything they've read. That's the actual "systematic" the brief asks for, made concrete.

**Why one workflow, not a list of tips:** this cohort already does ad hoc literature work with AI every day. A list of tips just reinforces the ad hoc habit. One concrete workflow, with a template they take away and reuse in week one, is what changes behavior from improvised to systematic.

## Structure (30 minutes)

| Time | Segment | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00-0:04 | Hook | Show the raw AI extraction from the worked example. It reads as completely correct. Ask the room what's wrong with it. Nobody catches it in 60 seconds. |
| 0:04-0:09 | Name the problem | Walk through the two real errors: the AI overstates how far a paper's result actually applies, and it mixes up two related but different terms. Name the pattern: AI summaries sound fluent and confident, and they fail quietly. |
| 0:09-0:20 | Live worked example | Run the actual three-step workflow on the same three papers, live, using the prompt library. Extract is fast and AI-driven. Verify means checking each claim against the source text, not the AI's own summary. Synthesize only runs on the corrected version, and produces a note that links back to each paper. |
| 0:20-0:26 | Hands-on | Fellows take the Verify step and run it against one claim from a paper in their own reading pile. Pair up, compare what they found. |
| 0:26-0:30 | Close | Hand out the template and the three prompts. One ask: try it on the next paper you read this week, and drop the note into your own vault, not eventually. |

**What fellows leave with:** the paper note template (with frontmatter and tags built in), the three prompts, and this worked example as a reference for what "verified" looks like next to "fluent," and for what a small linked vault looks like after just three papers.
